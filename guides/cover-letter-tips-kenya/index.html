How to Write a Cover Letter (Application Letter) in Kenya

By RemoteKazi Team · Updated 3 Nov 2026

A good cover letter can be the difference between two candidates with similar CVs. Most Kenyan cover letters are generic, too long and copied from the same templates, so a short, specific one stands out immediately. This guide shows you a simple structure, a full example, and the mistakes that get letters ignored.

Do you still need a cover letter?

Usually, yes. Many Kenyan employers, NGOs and government bodies ask for an "application letter" or "cover letter" with your CV. Even when it is optional, a good letter helps. It shows that you read the advert, explains why you want this particular job, and gives you a chance to address things your CV cannot, such as a career change or a gap.

When an online form has no space for a letter, the "summary" or "why are you interested" box does the same job. Use the same approach.

The ideal length

Keep it to one page, about 250 to 350 words, in three or four short paragraphs. Recruiters skim. A letter that fills two pages is rarely read to the end.

A simple structure

  1. Header: your name, phone number, email and the date. For a formal application letter, add the employer's name and address.
  2. Greeting: use the hiring manager's name if the advert gives it, such as "Dear Ms Achieng". Otherwise use "Dear Hiring Manager" or the title in the advert, such as "Dear Human Resource Manager".
  3. Opening paragraph: name the exact job, and say in one sentence why you are a strong fit.
  4. Middle paragraph(s): give two or three specific examples that match the advert's main requirements, with numbers where you can.
  5. Closing paragraph: say you would welcome an interview, and thank the reader.
  6. Sign-off: "Yours sincerely" if you used a name, "Yours faithfully" if you used "Dear Sir/Madam", followed by your full name.

A full example

Dear Hiring Manager,

Re: Application for the position of Accounts Assistant

I am writing to apply for the Accounts Assistant role advertised on your website. I am a CPA (Intermediate level) student with two years' experience in accounts payable at a Nairobi manufacturing company, and I would welcome the chance to bring that experience to your finance team.

In my current role, I process more than 300 supplier invoices a month in QuickBooks and prepare weekly payment schedules for approval. Last year I reconciled a six-month backlog of supplier statements, which recovered KSh 420,000 in duplicate payments. I also prepare monthly VAT schedules and work closely with our external auditors during the year-end audit.

Your advert mentions a need for strong reconciliation skills and experience with iTax. Both are a daily part of my work, and I am comfortable working to tight month-end deadlines.

I have attached my CV, and I would be glad to discuss how I could support your team. Thank you for considering my application.

Yours faithfully,
Brian Otieno
0700 000 000 · brian.otieno@example.com

Notice what the example does: it names the job, gives a clear reason to keep reading in the first paragraph, proves the claims with numbers, repeats the advert's own requirements, and stays short.

How to make your letter specific

  • Mirror the advert. Pick the two or three requirements that matter most and show that you meet each one.
  • Mention the employer by name, and say something real about why you want to work there, such as a programme they run or a product you use.
  • Use examples, not adjectives. "Hardworking and result-oriented" means nothing without proof. "Cut stock shortages by half across three branches" proves it.
  • Explain anything unusual briefly. If you are changing careers, relocating or returning after a break, one positive sentence is enough.

If you have little or no experience

Focus on what you have done, not what you lack. Draw examples from:

  • Your industrial attachment or internship
  • Your final-year project or a relevant course
  • Leadership roles in student clubs, church groups or community organisations
  • Part-time or casual work, which shows reliability and customer skills

Avoid apologising for being new. Phrases like "Although I have no experience..." draw attention to the gap. Show your enthusiasm and your ability to learn quickly through examples instead.

Common mistakes that get letters ignored

  • Repeating your CV line by line. The letter should explain and highlight, not list.
  • The wrong company or job name, left over from a previous application. Always double-check before sending.
  • Copying a template word for word. Recruiters recognise the popular ones instantly.
  • Long, emotional appeals. Explaining hardship or that you "badly need this job" does not help. Focus on what you can do for the employer.
  • Spelling and grammar errors. Read the letter aloud and ask someone else to check it.
  • Including personal details such as your ID number, age, religion or marital status, unless the advert asks for them.

Sending your application by email

Many Kenyan employers still ask for applications by email. When they do:

  • Use the exact subject line the advert asks for. If none is given, use the job title and reference number, for example "Application for Accounts Assistant – Ref: FIN/12/2026".
  • Either paste a short version of your letter into the email body, or write two or three lines and attach the full letter.
  • Attach your CV and letter as PDFs with clear file names, such as Brian-Otieno-Cover-Letter.pdf.
  • Send it from a professional email address, and check the attachments open before you send.

Final checklist

  • It names the exact job and the employer
  • It fits on one page
  • It includes at least two specific examples linked to the advert
  • It ends with a clear request for an interview
  • Names, dates and contact details are correct
  • Someone else has proofread it
