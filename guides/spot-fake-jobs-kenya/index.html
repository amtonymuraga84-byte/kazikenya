How to Spot a Fake Job Advert in Kenya

By RemoteKazi Team · Updated 3 Nov 2026

Job scams target people who are searching hardest for work, and in Kenya they are everywhere: in WhatsApp groups, on Facebook pages, in SMS messages and even on real job boards. A fake advert can cost you money, your personal data or, in the worst cases, your safety. This guide explains the warning signs, how to check an employer in a few minutes, and what to do if you have already been caught.

The golden rule

A real employer never asks you to pay to get a job. That includes fees for "registration", "processing", interviews, training, uniforms, medical tests, police clearance arranged by them, or "securing your slot". If anyone asks you for money before you are hired, stop. This one rule would prevent most job scams in Kenya.

The most common job scams in Kenya

1. Pay-to-apply and "processing fee" scams

The advert promises a well-paid job with a well-known company, NGO or government agency. After you apply, you are told you have been shortlisted, but must send KSh 500 to KSh 5,000 by M-Pesa for "registration" or "processing". After you pay, the contact stops replying, or asks for more.

2. Fake government and county recruitment

Scammers copy the names of the police, KDF, prisons, the TSC, county governments and state corporations. They claim to have "slots" or "connections" that can guarantee you a place, for a fee. Real public service recruitment is advertised on official websites and in newspapers, and is free to apply for. Nobody can sell you a guaranteed slot.

3. Overseas job scams

Jobs in the Gulf, Europe or Canada are advertised with high salaries, free flights and accommodation. Victims pay large sums for visas, medicals and "agency fees", only to find the job does not exist, or that they are sent abroad on a different contract with poor conditions. Before you deal with any agency for work abroad, check that it is on the list of licensed private employment agencies published by the National Employment Authority (NEA).

4. "Work from home" and task scams

You are offered easy online work: liking videos, rating hotels, typing or "data entry". At first you are paid small amounts to build trust. Then you are asked to "top up" or "unlock" higher-paying tasks with your own money, which you never get back.

5. Fake cheque and equipment scams

Common with remote jobs. After a quick text-only "interview", you are hired and told to buy equipment from a specific supplier, or you are sent a payment and asked to send part of it back. The original payment is later reversed, and you lose the money you sent.

6. Data harvesting

Some fake adverts are not after money at all, at least not straight away. They collect copies of IDs, KRA PINs, bank details and photos, which are later used to take out loans, open SIM cards or commit fraud in your name.

Red flags to watch for

  • You are asked to pay for anything before you start work.
  • Contact is only by WhatsApp, SMS or a personal email such as Gmail or Yahoo, with no company website or office address.
  • The pay is unrealistic for the work, for example KSh 80,000 a month for a general worker with no experience.
  • No interview, or a text-only interview, and you are "hired" immediately.
  • Pressure to act fast: "only three slots left", "pay before 5pm today".
  • Poor spelling and grammar, or an advert that mentions many different job titles at once.
  • Requests for sensitive data early, such as your M-Pesa PIN, bank PIN, or a photo of yourself holding your ID.
  • The phone number is registered to an individual. When you send money, the M-Pesa confirmation shows a person's name instead of a company or paybill.

How to check an employer in five minutes

  1. Find the official website. Search the company name yourself, rather than clicking a link in the advert.
  2. Look for the vacancy on the employer's own careers page. Large companies, NGOs and government bodies publish their real vacancies there. If it is not there, be suspicious.
  3. Check the email domain. A real Safaricom recruiter writes from an @safaricom.co.ke address, not @gmail.com. Be careful with near-copies such as "safaricom-careers.com".
  4. Call the office using a number you found yourself on the official website, and ask whether the vacancy is real.
  5. Search the name and phone number together with words like "scam" or "fake". Other victims often post warnings.
  6. For overseas jobs, confirm the agency is licensed by the NEA, and never hand over your passport.

Protect your personal information

  • Never share your M-Pesa PIN, bank PIN or one-time codes with anyone, for any reason.
  • Only share copies of your ID and certificates after you have confirmed the employer is real. Real employers usually ask for these at the interview or offer stage, not before.
  • When you do send ID copies, consider writing across them in pen what they are for, for example "For job application at XYZ Ltd only, 3 Nov 2026".
  • Do not put your ID number, date of birth or home address on your CV. Our CV guide explains what to include instead.

If you have already been scammed

  1. Stop contact and do not send any more money, even if they promise a refund after "one last fee".
  2. If you sent money by M-Pesa very recently, contact Safaricom immediately to request a reversal. Speed matters, because reversals are only possible before the money is withdrawn.
  3. Keep the evidence: screenshots of the advert, chats, phone numbers and M-Pesa messages.
  4. Report it to the police at your nearest station, or to the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI). Ask for an OB number.
  5. If your ID or documents were shared, watch for loans or SIM cards taken out in your name, and report any you find.
  6. Warn others. Report the advert to the platform where you saw it, and tell your WhatsApp groups.

Being scammed is not a sign of foolishness. These schemes are designed by professionals to exploit people who are under pressure. Reporting them helps protect the next person.
