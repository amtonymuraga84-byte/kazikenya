Can Kenyans Join the US Military? Requirements, Steps and Benefits Explained

Published 24 Oct 2026 · Updated 26 Oct 2026 · By RemoteKazi Team · 3 min read

A Kenyan-born member of the US military has gone viral after explaining how she enlisted, from failing her first fitness check to earning a large signing bonus. Here is who can actually join, the steps involved, and what Kenyans living in the US should know before speaking to a recruiter.

A US-based Kenyan has gone viral on TikTok after sharing how she joined the American military. In a video posted on October 21, 2026, the creator, known as @puremorigan, said she moved to the United States for nursing school and joined the armed forces to help pay for her studies and to speed up her path to US citizenship.

Her story resonated because it was honest. She did not pass on her first try: she failed the initial body-weight and fitness check, then trained for a full year until she met the standard.

Who can join?

This is the most important point, and it is often misunderstood: you cannot enlist from Kenya, and you cannot enlist on a student, visitor or tourist visa. To join the regular US armed forces you must be either:

  • a US citizen, or
  • a lawful permanent resident with a valid Green Card, living in the United States.

A special programme that once allowed some non-permanent residents with critical skills to enlist was suspended years ago. Be very wary of anyone in Kenya who offers to help you "join the US Army" for a fee; it is a common scam.

The steps to enlist

  1. Speak to a recruiter for the branch you are interested in: Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Space Force or Coast Guard. Recruiters are free and are the only official entry point.
  2. Take the ASVAB. The Armed Services Vocational Aptitude Battery tests maths, reading and technical knowledge. Your score decides which jobs you qualify for. The creator said her Kenyan secondary education gave her a strong foundation, even though she sat the test ten years after high school.
  3. Pass background checks. A criminal record, pending court cases or serious traffic offences can disqualify you.
  4. Pass medical and fitness checks at a Military Entrance Processing Station (MEPS), including height and weight standards.
  5. Choose your job and sign your contract. In-demand specialties can come with signing bonuses. The creator said she received a US$50,000 bonus, about KSh 6.4 million, four months after processing. Bonuses vary widely by role and year, and come with service commitments.

Benefits, and what to weigh up

Benefits include a regular salary, housing and food allowances, full medical cover, money for education, job training, and a faster route to US citizenship for eligible service members.

Things to consider: a contract of several years, the possibility of deployment, strict discipline, and time away from family. Talk to people who have served before you sign anything.

For Kenyans still at home

If you are in Kenya and interested in a uniformed career, look at local options too. The Kenya Defence Forces, National Police Service, Kenya Wildlife Service and Kenya Prisons Service all run recruitment drives. See our KDF vs UPDF salary comparison for what Kenyan soldiers earn.

For a legal route to the US, the only realistic options are study, a job offer with a work visa, family sponsorship or the annual Diversity Visa lottery, which is free to enter on the official US State Department website.
