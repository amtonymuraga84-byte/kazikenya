No More Mandatory IEBC Livestream: What the New Elections Law Means for 2027

Published 25 Oct 2026 · Updated 27 Oct 2026 · By RemoteKazi Team · 4 min read

The National Assembly has passed the Elections (Amendment) Bill, removing the IEBC's legal duty to livestream results as they are announced. Results declared at polling stations are still final, and media and observers can still broadcast them. Here is what changes for 2027.

Kenya's rules for announcing election results are changing ahead of the 2027 General Election. On October 22, 2026, the National Assembly passed the Elections (Amendment) Bill, which rewrites Section 39 of the Elections Act and removes the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission's (IEBC) legal duty to livestream results as they are announced.

The Bill came out of the bipartisan NADCO talks and was sponsored in the Senate by Aaron Cheruiyot and Stewart Madzayo.

What changes

The old law required the IEBC to set up a way to livestream polling station results. The new section drops that requirement. In practice, Kenyans lose the guarantee of an official IEBC live feed from the national tallying centre.

What stays the same

  • Polling station results are still final. The figures declared and signed at each polling station remain the official result for that station.
  • Presidential results must still be sent electronically within two hours.
  • Signed result forms will still be published on a public online portal, so anyone can check them.

Can results still be streamed?

Yes. The new law does not ban livestreaming. TV stations, election observers and party agents can still collect results from polling stations and broadcast them on their own. The difference is that the IEBC itself is no longer legally obliged to provide the live feed.

The debate in Parliament

Supporters of the change, including Deputy Speaker Gladys Boss Shollei, argued that the existing safeguards, such as electronic transmission and public result forms, are enough to protect the vote. An amendment by Suba South MP Caroli Omondi to restore the livestreaming requirement was defeated. Critics say the Bill sets no deadline for posting every polling station's result form online, which could leave gaps in what the public can see on results day.

How you can follow results in 2027

  • Check the IEBC's public results portal for scanned forms from your polling station.
  • Compare figures reported by several media houses rather than relying on one.
  • Look at the result form posted at your polling station after counting, and compare it with what is published online.

Election jobs to look out for

General elections are one of the biggest short-term employers in Kenya. Ahead of 2027, the IEBC is expected to recruit thousands of temporary staff, including presiding officers, deputy presiding officers, polling clerks and ICT support clerks. Media houses, observer missions and research firms also hire data clerks and enumerators.

These roles are advertised on the IEBC's official website and in national newspapers. Applying is free. We will post verified election vacancies on RemoteKazi as they open, so check the latest jobs or join our free alerts.

How to prepare for an IEBC temporary job

When the IEBC advertises election-period posts, competition is strong. Past adverts have typically asked applicants to:

  • Be Kenyan citizens with a national ID
  • Be registered voters, often in the area where they apply
  • Meet a minimum education level, which differs by role
  • Not be members of a political party or active in partisan politics

Read each advert carefully, because requirements and pay rates are set for each election. Successful applicants usually attend training before election day, and payment for temporary staff is generally made through bank or mobile money after the work is done. Keep your details accurate on the application, as the IEBC checks them.

Check your own voter registration

If you plan to vote in 2027, confirm your registration details well before the election. The IEBC publishes how to verify your registration on its official website. Mistakes in your details are much easier to fix early, during the official verification period, than on election day.

Beyond the IEBC

Election years also create work in media, research and data collection. Polling firms hire field interviewers, observer missions hire monitors, and newsrooms hire temporary data and results clerks. These jobs reward accuracy, punctuality and neutrality, which are worth highlighting on your CV.

Frequently asked questions

Does the new law ban livestreaming of results? No. It removes the IEBC's legal duty to provide a livestream. Media, observers and party agents can still broadcast results.

Are polling station results still final? Yes. Results declared and signed at the polling station remain the official result for that station.

How much do IEBC temporary staff earn? Rates are set by the IEBC for each election and differ by role. Check the official advert for the current rates and never pay anyone to secure a post.
