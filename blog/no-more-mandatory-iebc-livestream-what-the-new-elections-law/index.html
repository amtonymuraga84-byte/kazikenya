No More Mandatory IEBC Livestream: What the New Elections Law Means for 2027

Published 25 Oct 2026 · By RemoteKazi Team · 1 min read

The National Assembly has passed the Elections (Amendment) Bill, removing the IEBC’s legal duty to livestream results as they are announced at polling stations. Results declared at polling stations will still be final, and media houses and observers remain free to broadcast results independently.

On October 22, 2026, MPs approved the Bill, which replaces Section 39 of the Elections Act. The new section drops the requirement for the IEBC to set up a mechanism for livestreaming polling station results. The Bill, sponsored in the Senate by Aaron Cheruiyot and Stewart Madzayo, came out of the NADCO talks.

What Stays the Same

Results declared at polling stations remain final. Presidential results must be transmitted electronically within two hours. Signed result forms will still be posted on a public portal.

Can People Still Livestream? Yes. The law does not ban streaming. TV stations, observers and party agents can still collect and broadcast results on their own. What Kenyans lose is the guaranteed official IEBC feed at the national tallying centre.

Deputy Speaker Gladys Boss Shollei said existing safeguards are enough, while an amendment by Suba South MP Caroli Omondi to restore livestreaming was defeated. Critics warn the Bill sets no deadline for posting every polling station’s form online.
