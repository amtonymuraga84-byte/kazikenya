Nurse Salary in Kenya 2026: Job Groups, Allowances and the New Deal

Published 02 Nov 2026 · By RemoteKazi Team · 7 min read

Nurses in Kenya are paid a basic salary set by job group, plus allowances that can add a third or more to the payslip. The 43-day strike that ended in September 2026 raised two of those allowances. Here is how nurse pay works, what each level roughly earns, and what to check before you accept an offer.

"How much does a nurse earn in Kenya?" has no single answer. Two nurses with the same diploma can take home very different amounts depending on whether they work for a county, a national referral hospital, a private hospital or an NGO, which town they are posted to, and whether they are on permanent terms or a contract.

This guide explains the parts of a nurse's pay, the figures that have been published for each level, and the allowance changes agreed in 2026.

How nurse pay is built

Public sector nurses are paid on a scale set by the Salaries and Remuneration Commission (SRC). A payslip has two parts:

  • Basic salary, set by your job group. Each job group has a minimum and maximum, and you move up by annual increments.
  • Allowances, which usually include house allowance, commuter allowance, health risk allowance, uniform allowance, a nursing service allowance, and extraneous or call allowances for staff who work extra hours.

House allowance depends on where you are posted. SRC groups duty stations into clusters, with Nairobi paying the most, followed by the other large towns such as Mombasa, Kisumu and Nakuru, then the rest of the country. So the same Registered Nurse III earns more in gross pay in Nairobi than in a rural sub-county hospital.

Most county governments now use their own County Public Service Board (CPSB) grade numbers, but these map to the older civil service letters.

Registered nurses have historically run from Job Group H at entry up to Job Group P. A Kiambu County advert in August 2026 for 300 Registered Nurse III posts listed the grade as "CPSB 11 ('H')", on contract, with pay "as set by the SRC". It asked for a diploma in Kenya Registered Community Health Nursing or Registered Midwifery, plus registration and a valid practising licence from the Nursing Council of Kenya.

The same Uasin Gishu advert listed a house allowance of KSh 4,500 and a commuter allowance of KSh 4,000 for Registered Nurse III, which gives a sense of how much allowances add at entry level.

Private hospitals set their own pay. Self-reported figures on salary sites put registered nurses at large Nairobi private hospitals at roughly KSh 55,000 to 100,000 a month, but these are estimates. Faith-based and mission hospitals often pay close to, or below, the public scale. NGO roles in health programmes can pay more but are usually tied to a project and its funding period.

What changed in 2026

County nurses: the September return-to-work deal

County nurses went on strike on 29 July 2026. The strike was called off on 9 September after 43 days, when the Council of Governors and the Kenya National Union of Nurses and Midwives signed a return-to-work formula. The main pay terms reported were:

  • Health risk allowance up from KSh 3,850 to KSh 8,000 a month.
  • Uniform allowance up from KSh 10,000 to KSh 15,000.
  • Both reported as effective from 1 July 2025, which means arrears for the months already worked.
  • Nurses hired under the Universal Health Coverage (UHC) programme to be moved to permanent and pensionable terms from the 2026/27 financial year.
  • A collective bargaining agreement (CBA) to be concluded within 45 days of the deal.

Reports differ on how gratuity owed to UHC contract staff will be handled, so UHC nurses should confirm their own terms with their county or union branch.

Kenyatta National Hospital

In August 2026 SRC advised that KNH could raise its nurses' uniform allowance from KSh 15,000 to KSh 25,000 in phases, and the monthly nursing service allowance from KSh 20,000 to KSh 30,000, subject to available funds. KNH and the union then signed a 2025–2029 CBA, reported in October 2026, with the new nursing service and uniform allowances taking effect from 1 September 2026. These terms apply to KNH staff only, not to county nurses.

Nursing service and extraneous allowances come on top. From the gross, expect deductions for PAYE, NSSF, the Social Health Insurance Fund (SHIF) and the Affordable Housing Levy. Use this only to compare offers. Your actual figures will be on your appointment letter.

How to raise your pay as a nurse

  1. Upgrade your qualification. Moving from a certificate to a diploma, or from a diploma to a BSc, moves you onto a higher entry grade.
  2. Specialise. Critical care, theatre, renal, oncology and midwifery skills are in demand and are often listed as an advantage in adverts.
  3. Keep your licence current. An expired Nursing Council of Kenya licence can lock you out of new posts and promotions.
  4. Read the terms, not just the basic. A contract with a lower basic but a nursing service allowance can beat a higher basic with few allowances. Ask whether the job is permanent, contract or locum. Our guide on negotiating salary in Kenya covers how to ask.
  5. Watch for fake recruitment. Genuine county and hospital adverts never charge an application or "processing" fee. See how to spot fake jobs.
