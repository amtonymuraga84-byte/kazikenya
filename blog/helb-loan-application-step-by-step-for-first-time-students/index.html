HELB Loan Application Step by Step: A Guide for First-Time University and TVET Students

Published 17 Nov 2026 · By RemoteKazi Team · 5 min read

Starting university or a TVET college and need help with fees? This guide explains how first-time applicants apply for HELB funding through the HEF Student Portal, the documents to prepare, how to avoid fake agents, and what to confirm on the official site before you start.

Paying for university or a TVET course is one of the biggest money decisions a young Kenyan faces. The Higher Education Loans Board (HELB) is the government body most students turn to, and the application is done online, by you, for free. This guide walks you through the process for first-time applicants and shows what to prepare so you do not lose weeks to avoidable mistakes.

What HELB funding is

For students joining university or TVET colleges, funding is now handled through the Higher Education Financing (HEF) system, which brings together HELB, the Universities Fund and the TVET funding body on one portal. According to HEF, support is based on need and can combine a scholarship, a loan and a household contribution. Need is assessed with a Means Testing Instrument, which looks at your family's circumstances.

In general, HEF explains that students placed in public universities through KUCCPS and TVET trainees under the Ministry of Education can receive scholarships and loans, while students in private universities or self-sponsored programmes can receive loans only. The amount you get depends on your assessed need and the cost of your programme. HEF does not publish a single fixed figure, so be wary of anyone who promises a specific amount before you apply.

Applying is voluntary and is done each academic year.

Before you start

HEF states that you need an official admission letter from the university or TVET institution where you were placed before you apply. So do not start until you have your admission or placement letter.

Documents and details to gather

The HEF website lists what first-time undergraduate and TVET applicants need. Prepare these in advance, scanned clearly where a file is required:

  • A valid email address and phone number
  • Your KCPE and KCSE index numbers and the year you sat the exams
  • A passport-size photo (jpeg, jpg or png)
  • A copy of your National ID or Maisha Card, both sides, as a PDF
  • Valid bank details or an M-Pesa number registered in your own name and ID number
  • Your parents' National ID numbers and registered phone numbers
  • A death certificate (PDF) if a parent is deceased
  • Your birth certificate (PDF) if you are applying as a minor
  • Two guarantors' ID numbers and registered phone numbers (these can be your parents)
  • A copy of your sponsorship letter (PDF) if you were sponsored in secondary school

Check that the names on your documents match your ID exactly. Mismatches are a common reason for delays.

Step-by-step: how to apply

  1. Go to the official portal. Type the address of the HEF website, hef.co.ke, into your browser yourself and use the Student Portal link from there. Avoid links sent on WhatsApp, social media or by agents.
  2. Create or log in to your account. If you already have an account from an earlier application, use it and update your details rather than creating a second one.
  3. Complete your profile. Fill in your personal details yourself, accurately. HEF stresses that you should fill in the application form yourself.
  4. Upload your documents in the formats requested.
  5. Choose the first-time application for undergraduate or TVET funding and enter the guarantor and bank or M-Pesa details.
  6. Review everything, then submit. Save the confirmation you receive.
  7. Track your status by logging in to the portal. Funds are normally paid at the start of the semester, with tuition going to your institution and upkeep, where it applies, to your account.

Is the application window open?

HEF announces a window for each academic year, and it can close or be extended. Reports in July 2026 said applications for 2026/2027 first-time applicants had opened, but sources disagreed on the closing date, and we could not confirm a current deadline on the official pages. Open the portal and check the notice on the day you plan to apply. If you are late, look at the HEF notices for any extension or the arrangements for later applicants.

Avoid scams

HEF states clearly that its services are free, and its site warns students not to fall for online fraud. Nobody can speed up your application for a fee, and a "cyber café agent" who asks for your portal password is a risk to your data and your loan. Never share your password or one-time codes. If something looks wrong, contact HELB through its official contact centre listed on the HEF site.

Tips for a smooth application

Apply as early as the window allows. Use a personal email address you will keep for years, because HELB loans are repaid later and you will need access to your account. Make sure the phone number and M-Pesa line are registered in your own name. Talk to your parents or guardians before you start, because you will need their ID details and their agreement to act as guarantors.

Frequently asked questions

Do I pay to apply? No. HEF states that its services are free.

Can I apply without an admission letter? HEF says an official admission letter from your institution is required before applying.

Can I apply if I am under 18? HEF's guidance says minors can apply using their KCSE index number and need a bank account in their own name. Check the portal for the current rules.

How much will I get? It depends on your assessed level of need and the cost of your programme. Check the official site for current amounts.

When do I start repaying? Check the HELB website for the current repayment terms and interest rate before you sign or accept any offer.

Do I need a KRA PIN? Requirements can change, so confirm on the official portal. Our guide to getting a KRA PIN online explains the special PIN for students who have no income.
