Karen Nyamu's Police Recruitment Remarks Under Investigation: How to Join the Police the Right Way

Published 25 Oct 2026 · Updated 26 Oct 2026 · By RemoteKazi Team · 2 min read

The National Police Service has opened an investigation after Nominated Senator Karen Nyamu described trying to help a supporter join the police despite him not meeting the requirements. Here is what was said, what the police said, and how to join the service honestly and safely.

The National Police Service (NPS) has opened an investigation into comments made by Nominated Senator Karen Nyamu about police recruitment.

What was said

In a media interview, Senator Nyamu described trying to help a loyal campaign supporter join the police service even though he did not meet the academic requirements. According to her account, the recruit got as far as the Kiganjo Police Training College before forged documents were discovered, and he was dismissed along with eight others.

How the police responded

In a statement on October 24, 2026, the NPS described the remarks as uncalled for, irresponsible and unacceptable, and said it had launched an investigation to establish the facts and whether any offence was committed. The service acknowledged that forged academic certificates have been found in past recruitment drives, but noted that the senator's remarks do not by themselves establish that she broke the law. The investigation is ongoing.

Why it matters to job seekers

Every police recruitment drive attracts far more applicants than there are places. When people believe that slots can be obtained through connections or payments, it hurts the honest candidates who meet the requirements and turn up on the day. It also feeds a thriving scam economy: fraudsters regularly charge desperate young people tens of thousands of shillings for "guaranteed" places that do not exist.

The case is also a reminder that forged certificates are eventually found. Recruits who use them can be dismissed, even after training, and may face criminal charges.

How police recruitment really works

Requirements are set out in each official advert and can change, so always read the latest one. They typically include:

  • Kenyan citizenship and a national ID card
  • An age limit
  • A minimum KCSE mean grade, with specific grades in subjects such as English or Kiswahili
  • Physical fitness and medical tests on recruitment day
  • A clean criminal record and good conduct

Recruitment is advertised by the National Police Service in national newspapers and on its official website and social media pages. The advert lists the date and the recruitment centres across the country where candidates should report.

Protect yourself

  • Recruitment is free. Never pay anyone, including officers, politicians or "agents", to secure a slot.
  • Use your real certificates. KNEC certificates can be verified, and forgery is a crime.
  • Report bribery requests to the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) or the Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA).
  • Prepare properly. Train for the fitness tests in the weeks before recruitment and bring all original documents.
