Who Checked the Blueprints? The NCA Managers Facing Heat Over Eastleigh’s 16-Storey Collapse

Published 24 Oct 2026 · By RemoteKazi Team · 1 min read

National Construction Authority (NCA) leadership faces intense scrutiny following the collapse of a partially built 16-storey building in Eastleigh, Nairobi, on October 20, 2026. The disaster occurred just seven weeks after a new management team assumed office, triggering a multi-agency probe announced by CS Kipchumba Murkomen.

The collapse of the 16-storey structure in Eastleigh came merely seven weeks after senior officials took charge at the National Construction Authority (NCA) following the departure of former head Maurice Akech. Top management, led by Acting Executive Director Susan C. Deritu (who assumed office on September 2, 2026), is now facing hard questions regarding registration protocols, site compliance, and quality control.

Police authorities have warned that individuals who approved or oversaw the project's construction could face criminal negligence charges if found culpable.

Several key NCA managers responsible for regulatory compliance are central to the investigation: Raymond Kraal: Manager for Registration, who oversees project entry into authority records. Stephen Mukuha: Manager for Compliance, tasked with post-registration monitoring and enforcement. Paul Mwangi: Manager for Quality Assurance and Planning, who manages internal inspection systems. Sampson Lukoba: Corporation Secretary and Head of Legal, who advises on enforcement boundaries and legal distinctions between county approvals and NCA registration.
