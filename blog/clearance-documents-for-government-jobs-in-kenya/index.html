Chapter Six Clearance Documents for Government Jobs in Kenya: What You Need and How to Get Each One

Published 26 Oct 2026 · By RemoteKazi Team · 4 min read

Almost every government, county and parastatal job advert in Kenya asks shortlisted candidates for five clearance documents to show they meet Chapter Six of the Constitution. Here is what each one is, how to get it, what it costs and how long to allow, so a missing document never costs you a job.

If you have read a government job advert in Kenya, you have probably seen a line like this: applicants must satisfy the requirements of Chapter Six of the Constitution and provide valid copies of the following. Chapter Six is the part of the Constitution on leadership and integrity, and employers use these documents to check it.

A recent public-sector advert, for example, asked for:

  1. A certificate of good conduct from the DCI
  2. A tax compliance certificate from KRA
  3. A clearance or compliance certificate from HELB
  4. A clearance from the EACC
  5. A credit report from an approved CRB

Many county governments, parastatals, universities and large private employers ask for the same set, especially for management roles. Some only ask for them once you are shortlisted, but they often give you very little time, so it pays to have them ready.

1. Certificate of good conduct (DCI)

What it shows: whether you have a criminal record in Kenya.

How to get it: apply on eCitizen under the Directorate of Criminal Investigations, pay the fee (reported at KSh 1,050 including the eCitizen charge), print the form, and have your fingerprints taken at a DCI office or Huduma Centre. You download the certificate from eCitizen when it is ready.

Allow: several weeks. This is usually the slowest document, so start here. Full steps: how to get a certificate of good conduct.

2. Tax compliance certificate (KRA)

What it shows: that you have filed your tax returns and have no outstanding tax.

How to get it: log in to iTax with your KRA PIN and apply for a Tax Compliance Certificate. If all your returns are filed and taxes paid, it is usually issued quickly and can be downloaded. If you have missing returns, including nil returns from years when you earned nothing, you must file them first, and there may be penalties to clear.

Allow: minutes if you are up to date; longer if you need to file old returns. Don't have a PIN yet? See how to get a KRA PIN online.

3. HELB clearance or compliance certificate

What it shows: whether you have a HELB loan and, if so, whether you are repaying it as agreed.

How to get it: apply through the HELB portal (or eCitizen). There are three types:

  • Non-loanee compliance certificate if you never took a HELB loan
  • Loanee compliance certificate if you have a loan and are up to date with repayments
  • Clearance certificate if you have fully repaid your loan

If you are behind on repayments, you will need to agree a repayment plan with HELB first. HELB's fees for these certificates have changed over the years, so check the current cost on the portal.

4. EACC clearance

What it shows: that the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission has no integrity concerns about you.

How it works: this one is different. According to the EACC, you fill in a Self-Declaration Form in two copies, sign it in front of a Commissioner for Oaths or Magistrate, and attach copies of your ID, KRA PIN certificate and academic certificates. You submit it at the EACC head office in Nairobi or a regional office, or apply online through the EACC's Adili portal.

You receive a stamped copy as acknowledgement. The EACC does not issue you a certificate; it reports directly to the employer about shortlisted candidates. The EACC charges no fee for the form, though the Commissioner for Oaths may charge for witnessing it.

5. Credit report or clearance (CRB)

What it shows: your credit history, including whether you have unpaid loans that have been reported, including digital loans.

How to get it: apply to a licensed credit reference bureau such as TransUnion, Metropol or Creditinfo through their website or app. Employers usually want a clearance certificate or credit report, which comes with a fee (one 2024 guide put it at about KSh 2,200; check current prices). If you are negatively listed, clear or settle the debt with the lender first; the lender then updates the bureau.

Allow: a few days, longer if you need to clear a listing first.

A simple plan

  • Start with the DCI certificate. It takes the longest.
  • Check your KRA and HELB status now, and fix any missed returns or repayments before they become a problem.
  • Keep a folder (paper and on your phone or email) with current copies of all five, plus your ID, KRA PIN certificate and academic certificates.
  • Watch expiry dates. Many employers want certificates issued within the last year or less. Note when each one was issued.
  • Never pay "agents" who claim they can get clearances faster or "fix" your record. Apply only through the official portals.
