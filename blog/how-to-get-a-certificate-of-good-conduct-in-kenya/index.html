How to Get a Certificate of Good Conduct in Kenya (2026 Step-by-Step Guide)

Published 26 Oct 2026 · By RemoteKazi Team · 3 min read

Most employers in Kenya, and every government job, ask for a certificate of good conduct (police clearance certificate) from the DCI. Here is exactly how to apply on eCitizen, what it costs, where to take your fingerprints, how long it takes, and the mistakes that cause delays.

A certificate of good conduct, officially called a police clearance certificate, confirms whether you have a criminal record in Kenya. It is issued by the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) and is one of the most requested documents in Kenyan job applications. Government jobs require it, and many private employers, schools, banks and recruitment agencies ask for it too. You may also need one for visas, work permits abroad and some licences.

Because it can take several weeks to arrive, it is worth applying before you see the job you want.

What you need

  • Your original national ID (and a copy)
  • An eCitizen account linked to your ID
  • A phone number and email address you can access
  • The fee, payable by M-Pesa or card through eCitizen

Step-by-step: applying on eCitizen

  1. Log in to eCitizen. Go to the official eCitizen portal (accounts.ecitizen.go.ke) and sign in, or create an account using your national ID details.
  2. Choose the DCI service. From your dashboard, open the Directorate of Criminal Investigations and select Police Clearance Certificate.
  3. Fill in the form. Enter your personal details carefully. Your names must match your ID exactly; mismatches are a common cause of delays.
  4. Pay the fee. Independent guides updated in September 2026 put the total at KSh 1,050 (KSh 1,000 for the certificate plus a KSh 50 eCitizen convenience fee). Always confirm the amount shown on the eCitizen payment page before you pay. Payments are not refundable.
  5. Print the application form and invoice. After payment, download and print the form (often called the C24 form) and your payment receipt.
  6. Book and attend your fingerprint appointment. Take the printed form, your original ID and a copy to the DCI office or Huduma Centre offering fingerprinting. Your fingerprints are captured there.
  7. Wait for processing. You will get an SMS or email when your certificate is ready.
  8. Download your certificate. Log in to eCitizen and download the PDF. Some offices also allow collection in person.

How long does it take?

Processing times vary. Recent guides report anywhere from about two weeks to six weeks after fingerprints are taken, and it can take longer during busy recruitment seasons, such as when large government hiring drives are open. Apply as early as you can.

How long is it valid?

A certificate of good conduct is generally treated as valid for 12 months from the date of issue. Some employers and embassies set their own rules, for example accepting only a certificate issued in the last three or six months, so always check what the employer asks for.

Applying from abroad

Kenyans living outside the country can apply on eCitizen too. Fingerprints are usually taken at a Kenyan embassy or high commission, or at a local police station that can capture them, and then submitted as directed by the DCI.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using an unofficial website or agent. Apply only through the official eCitizen portal. Never pay anyone who promises to "speed up" your certificate.
  • Name mismatches between your eCitizen account, your ID and the form.
  • Forgetting the printed form on fingerprint day.
  • Waiting for a job advert before applying, then missing the deadline.

Other documents employers ask for

Government and many senior private-sector jobs also ask for a KRA tax compliance certificate, HELB clearance, an EACC clearance and a CRB report. See our guide to clearance documents for government jobs, and our guide on how to get a KRA PIN online.
