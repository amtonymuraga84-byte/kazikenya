Step-by-Step: US-Based Kenyan TikToker Breaks Down Enlistment Steps and Qualifications for Joining the American Military

Published 24 Oct 2026 · By RemoteKazi Team · 2 min read

A US-based Kenyan service member has gained widespread online traction after sharing a detailed video breakdown on how non-citizens and diaspora members can enlist in the United States military. From overcoming strict physical fitness tests to securing up to $50,000 in signing bonuses, her viral guide provides specific steps and entry requirements

In a video posted on October 21, 2026, TikTok user @puremorigan detailed her transition from leaving Kenya for nursing school in the United States to joining the US armed forces. While her main goal was securing financial support for her studies and acquiring American citizenship, her journey hit an immediate roadblock when she failed the initial body weight and physical fitness evaluation at recruitment.

Rather than quitting, she spent an entire year dedicated to rigorous fitness training to meet the military’s body composition standards.

"I worked out hard because I really, really, really wanted this," she shared, emphasizing that physical persistence is essential for prospective recruits.

For foreign-born candidates and diaspora residents, the TikToker highlighted five core prerequisite steps needed to successfully enlist across military branches (Army, Navy, Air Force, or Marines):

Legal Residency Status: Applicants must possess a valid Green Card (Permanent Resident Card) or hold U.S. citizenship. Enlistment is not available to those on temporary visitor, student, or tourist visas.

Contacting Branch Recruiters: Candidates must directly approach an official liaison or recruiter for their preferred military branch (e.g., Army, Navy, Air Force).

Academic Evaluation (ASVAB Test): Candidates must pass the Armed Services Vocational Aptitude Battery (ASVAB) exam administered at a Military Entrance Processing Station (MEPS). She noted that Kenya's high school curriculum provided a strong foundation that helped her pass the test despite having completed secondary school ten years prior.

Background Checks and Legal Records: Military entry requires a clean record. Severe traffic infractions, pending court cases, or criminal records can immediately disqualify an applicant.

Physical & Medical Verification: Recruits must clear comprehensive medical and fitness checks at MEPS before receiving an official enlistment contract.

Highlighting the tangible incentives of military service, she revealed that selecting in-demand specialties can come with lucrative sign-on perks. Just four months after completing her initial processing, she secured a $50,000 signing bonus (approximately KSh 6.4 million).

She encouraged eligible Green Card holders looking for career growth, fully covered healthcare, educational benefits, and an expedited path to U.S. citizenship to consider military service as a structured career path.
