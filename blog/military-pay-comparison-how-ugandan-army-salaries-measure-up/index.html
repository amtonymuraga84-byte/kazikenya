KDF vs UPDF Salaries: How Kenyan and Ugandan Soldiers' Pay Compares in 2026

Published 22 Oct 2026 · Updated 27 Oct 2026 · By RemoteKazi Team · 4 min read

A major pay rise in Uganda has narrowed the salary gap between new soldiers in the Uganda People's Defence Forces (UPDF) and the Kenya Defence Forces (KDF). Here is how base pay compares rank by rank, what the figures leave out, and what to know if you are thinking about a military career.

Uganda has overhauled pay for its armed forces, and the change has closed much of the gap between what new soldiers earn there and in Kenya. For young people weighing a career in uniform, here is how the numbers compare.

Uganda's pay rise

After the review, a private in the Uganda People's Defence Forces (UPDF) now earns a base salary of about 704,000 Ugandan shillings a month, roughly KSh 26,100. That is a big jump from about KSh 17,300 the previous year. It is still below the KSh 41,500 that Uganda's Parliament had recommended; the Ministry of Defence cited budget limits for the 2026/27 financial year.

Rank-by-rank comparison

The table below compares base monthly pay, converted to Kenya shillings. KDF figures are ranges from pay scales published in August 2026, because pay rises with years of service within each rank.

Corporal / Sergeantabout KSh 41,000 (sergeant)KSh 32,250 – 70,000 (corporal)
Captainabout KSh 97,600KSh 73,182 – 110,000

What the numbers tell us

  • At entry level, the two forces are now close. A new UPDF private earns about the same as a KDF private in the middle of the pay band.
  • Kenya's ranges are wider. Long-serving KDF personnel can earn well above their Ugandan counterparts at the same rank, while new entrants may earn less.
  • Base pay is only part of the package. Both forces pay allowances for things like housing, risk, deployment and special duties. Peacekeeping missions abroad can add a significant amount. Free or subsidised housing, food, medical cover and a pension also make a big difference to what a soldier actually takes home.
  • Cost of living differs. The same amount of money goes further in some places than others, so salaries are not a perfect like-for-like comparison.

Thinking of joining the KDF?

The KDF runs recruitment drives, usually once a year, for general service recruits, tradesmen and women, specialist officers and cadets. Requirements change with each drive, so always read the official advert, but they typically cover:

  • Kenyan citizenship, with a national ID card
  • An age limit, which differs between general recruits and specialists
  • A minimum KCSE grade, plus specific grades for some trades and specialist roles
  • Medical and physical fitness tests, and a clean criminal record

Recruitment adverts are published on the Ministry of Defence website and in national newspapers, and recruitment takes place at announced centres across the country.

Recruitment is free. Anyone who asks you for money to "secure a slot" in the KDF, police or any other service is a fraudster. Read our guide on how to spot a fake job advert.

How to prepare for KDF recruitment

  • Start training early. Fitness tests are a common reason candidates are turned away. Build up running endurance and basic strength over several weeks.
  • Get your documents in order: original national ID, original KCSE certificate or result slip, school leaving certificate and other documents the advert lists, plus copies.
  • Check the requirements for your category. General duty recruits, tradesmen and women, specialists and cadets each have different age and grade requirements.
  • Go to the right centre on the right day as listed in the official advert.
  • Report anyone asking for money. KDF recruitment is free, and paying a "fixer" can get you disqualified and lose you your money.

Comparing careers in uniform

If you are weighing the KDF against other services such as the police, prisons or wildlife service, compare more than the starting salary:

  • Allowances and housing, which can add significantly to take-home pay
  • Training and qualifications you will gain, including trades and degrees some services sponsor
  • Promotion paths and how long they typically take
  • Deployment and family life, including how often you may be posted away from home
  • Pension and life after service, since many personnel retire young enough for a second career

Skills gained in the forces, such as discipline, driving, engineering trades, logistics and medical training, are valued by private employers in security, transport and construction.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a KDF private earn? According to pay scales published in August 2026, a KDF private's base salary ranges from about KSh 19,941 to KSh 30,000 a month, before allowances.

How often does the KDF recruit? The KDF usually runs a recruitment drive about once a year. Dates, centres and requirements are published in an official advert.

Do I pay anything to join the KDF? No. KDF recruitment is free. Report anyone who asks you for money.
