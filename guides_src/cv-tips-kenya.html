Recruiters in Kenya often get hundreds of applications for one advert, and many now screen CVs with applicant tracking software before a person reads them. Most CVs are rejected in under a minute. This guide shows you how to write one that is easy to read, easy to match to the job, and honest enough to defend in an interview.

What a recruiter looks for in the first 30 seconds

A recruiter skimming your CV wants quick answers to three questions: can this person do the job, have they done something similar before, and do they meet the minimum requirements in the advert? If those answers are not visible on the first half of page one, your CV usually goes into the "no" pile, however good the rest is.

So the most important rule is simple: put the most relevant information first, and write it in the same words the advert uses.

The right length

  • Fresh graduates and early-career applicants: one page, two at most.
  • Three or more years of experience: two pages.
  • Senior, academic or technical roles: up to three pages is acceptable if every line is relevant.

Cut anything older than about ten years unless it is directly relevant to the job. A recruiter hiring an accountant does not need to know about your first holiday job.

A structure that works

Use this order. Plain headings help both people and software find what they need.

  1. Name and contact details: full name, phone number, a professional email address, your town (for example "Nakuru, Kenya") and a LinkedIn link if you have one.
  2. Professional summary: three or four lines saying who you are, your strongest experience and what you are looking for.
  3. Key skills: six to ten skills taken from the advert, where they genuinely describe you.
  4. Work experience: most recent job first, with job title, employer, town and dates (month and year).
  5. Education: highest qualification first. Include your degree class or diploma grade. Early-career applicants can add their KCSE mean grade.
  6. Certifications and training: CPA, ACCA, CCNA, first aid, driving licence class, and similar.
  7. Referees: two or three, ideally former supervisors or lecturers.

Writing a summary that gets read

Your summary should be specific to the job. Avoid lines such as "hardworking, honest team player seeking a challenging position", which every recruiter has read a thousand times.

Weak: "A dedicated and result-oriented individual looking for an opportunity to grow."
Strong: "Customer service agent with three years in a Nairobi call centre handling 60+ calls a day for a telco. Fluent in English and Swahili, trained in complaint handling, and looking for a team leader role."

Show results, not duties

Most Kenyan CVs list duties copied from a job description: "responsible for handling customer queries". That tells the recruiter what you were supposed to do, not how well you did it. Instead, describe what changed because of your work.

Instead ofWrite
Responsible for sales in Kisumu regionGrew monthly sales in Kisumu region from KSh 1.2M to KSh 1.8M in one year
Handled customer queriesResolved 40+ customer queries a day with a 95% satisfaction score
Did data entryCleaned and entered 3,000 patient records into a new system two weeks ahead of schedule
Attended to studentsTaught Form 3 Chemistry; class mean improved from 4.2 to 5.6 in one year

Only use numbers you can explain in an interview. Recruiters do ask, and a figure you cannot defend will cost you the job.

Match every CV to the advert

Sending the same CV to every job is the most common reason people get no replies. Before you apply:

  • Read the advert and underline the requirements and key words, such as "IFRS", "QuickBooks", "stakeholder engagement" or "Class BCE licence".
  • Make sure those words appear in your skills and experience, wherever they truthfully describe you.
  • Move the most relevant job or project higher up.
  • If the advert asks for a minimum qualification or years of experience, make it obvious that you meet it.

Make it readable for screening software

Applicant tracking systems can struggle with fancy layouts. To be safe:

  • Use a simple single-column layout and a standard font such as Arial or Calibri, size 10 to 12.
  • Avoid tables, text boxes, columns, icons, skill-rating bars and graphics.
  • Use standard headings: Experience, Education, Skills.
  • Save as PDF unless the employer asks for Word, and name the file clearly, for example Jane-Wanjiku-CV.pdf.

What to leave out

Many Kenyan CV templates still include personal details that employers do not need and that can lead to unfair judgement:

  • ID number, date of birth, marital status, religion, tribe and number of children
  • A photo, unless the advert asks for one
  • Your current salary
  • A long list of hobbies. One relevant line is enough, for example volunteering or leading a church youth group.

Government and county jobs are different: their application forms usually ask for your ID number and other details directly, so you fill those in on the form rather than the CV.

No experience yet? What to include

If you are a recent graduate, your CV can still show what you can do:

  • Attachment and internship: treat them as jobs and describe what you achieved.
  • Projects: your final-year project, a website you built, a business you ran or a survey you conducted.
  • Leadership: class representative, club official or student union roles.
  • Casual and part-time work: shop attendant, M-Pesa agent or tutoring all show reliability and customer skills.
  • Short courses: free online certificates in Excel, digital marketing or data analysis show initiative.

Referees

Kenyan employers usually expect two or three referees with their name, title, organisation, phone number and email. Choose people who supervised your work, not relatives or friends. Always ask permission first, and tell them which jobs you have applied for so they are ready when the call comes. You can also write "Referees available on request" if space is tight, but have the details ready.

Common mistakes that get CVs rejected

  • Spelling errors, or the wrong company name from a previous application
  • An unprofessional email address. Create one with your name, such as jane.wanjiku@...
  • Unexplained gaps. One honest line is enough, for example "2024: Full-time carer for a family member" or "2025: Ran a small poultry business".
  • Exaggerated or false qualifications. Employers verify certificates, and lying can lead to dismissal later.
  • Sending your CV as a photo or a scanned picture

Final checklist before you click "Apply"

  • The first half of page one shows clearly that you meet the advert's main requirements
  • Your summary is written for this job
  • Each role shows at least one result, not just duties
  • Your phone number and email are correct and working
  • Someone else has proofread it
  • The file is a PDF with your name in the file name
