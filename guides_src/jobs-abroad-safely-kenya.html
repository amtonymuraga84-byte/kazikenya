Every year, many Kenyans take up jobs abroad, in the Gulf, Europe, North America and elsewhere, as nurses, caregivers, drivers, hospitality workers, domestic workers and skilled professionals. Working abroad can transform a family's finances. But overseas recruitment also attracts fraud and exploitation, and some workers lose their savings or end up in unsafe conditions. This guide explains how to find genuine jobs abroad and protect yourself at every step.

Popular routes to work abroad

  • Licensed recruitment agencies that place workers with employers overseas
  • Government-supported labour mobility programmes, under agreements between Kenya and other countries
  • Direct applications to employers abroad, common for skilled professionals such as nurses, engineers and IT specialists
  • Skilled worker visa routes in countries with labour shortages, especially in health and care

Step 1: Use only licensed recruitment agencies

In Kenya, private employment agencies that recruit for jobs abroad must be registered and accredited by the National Employment Authority (NEA), which publishes a list of licensed agencies on its website. Before you deal with any agency:

  • Check that it appears on the NEA's current list of accredited agencies.
  • Visit its physical office, and confirm that the name and address match the NEA list.
  • Ask which employer you will work for, and look the employer up yourself.
  • Ask other workers who have travelled through the agency about their experience.

Be very careful with "agents" who operate only through WhatsApp or Facebook, or individuals who claim to have connections abroad.

Step 2: Understand the costs

Ask for every cost in writing before you pay anything, and find out which costs the employer covers. Legitimate costs you may have to pay for yourself can include your passport, some medical tests, and certificates such as a certificate of good conduct. Be suspicious of large, unexplained "processing" or "placement" fees, and never pay cash without an official receipt. Pay through traceable methods, ideally to the agency's registered business account rather than a personal number.

Step 3: Read your contract carefully

You should receive a written contract before you travel. Make sure it states clearly:

  • The employer's name and address, and your job title and duties
  • Your salary, how and when it will be paid, and any deductions
  • Working hours, rest days and annual leave
  • Accommodation, food, medical cover and transport
  • Contract length, and who pays for your return ticket

Keep copies of your contract, both on paper and on your phone, and share them with a trusted family member. Do not accept a "new" contract with worse terms after you arrive.

Step 4: Get the right visa

Make sure you travel on a valid work visa or work permit for the job, not a tourist or visit visa. Working on a visit visa is illegal in most countries and leaves you with no legal protection. Check visa requirements on the official government website or embassy of the country you are going to.

Step 5: Protect your documents

  • Never hand over your passport to an agent or employer to keep. In many countries it is illegal for employers to hold workers' passports.
  • Keep scanned copies of your passport, visa, contract and certificates in your email or cloud storage.
  • Leave copies with your family in Kenya.

Red flags of overseas job scams

  • An unusually high salary for a simple job, with no interview
  • Pressure to pay quickly to "secure" a visa or flight
  • An agency that is not on the NEA list, or that has no office
  • Being asked to travel on a tourist visa and "change it later"
  • No written contract, or a contract in a language you cannot read
  • Being told not to tell anyone about the job or the agency

Before you travel

  • Register with the Kenyan embassy or consulate in the country you are going to, where possible, and save its contact details.
  • Learn the basics of the local labour laws and your rights as a worker.
  • Join reputable support groups for Kenyans in that country.
  • Agree with your family how and how often you will stay in touch.

If something goes wrong abroad

If your employer withholds your pay, takes your passport, or you feel unsafe, contact the Kenyan embassy or consulate in that country as soon as possible. Report problems with a recruitment agency to the National Employment Authority, and report fraud to the police in Kenya. Keep records of everything: messages, payments and your contract.

Skilled professionals: apply directly

If you are a nurse, doctor, engineer, IT professional or other skilled worker, many employers abroad hire directly through their own websites and international job boards, without an agency. You will usually need to have your qualifications recognised and may need an English language test. Applying directly often costs less and gives you more control over the process.
