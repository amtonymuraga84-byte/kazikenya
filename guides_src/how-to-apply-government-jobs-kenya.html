Government jobs are some of the most sought-after in Kenya, thanks to job security, pension benefits and clear salary scales. They also attract huge numbers of applicants, and many people are rejected for simple mistakes such as a missing document or an application sent to the wrong place. This guide explains where government jobs are advertised, how the application process works, and what documents you need.

Who hires for government jobs in Kenya?

Public sector jobs are filled by several different bodies, and each has its own process:

  • The Public Service Commission (PSC) recruits for national government ministries and state departments.
  • County Public Service Boards recruit for each of the 47 county governments, from health workers to administrators.
  • The Teachers Service Commission (TSC) recruits and deploys teachers in public schools.
  • State corporations and parastatals, such as Kenya Power, KenGen, KEMRI, the Kenya Ports Authority and Kenya Wildlife Service, run their own recruitment.
  • Public universities and hospitals recruit their own academic, medical and support staff.
  • Security services, such as the National Police Service and the Kenya Defence Forces, hold separate recruitment drives with their own requirements.

Where government jobs are advertised

  • The official website of the commission, county or organisation that is hiring. This is always the most reliable source.
  • National newspapers, including the government's MyGov pull-out.
  • Official social media accounts of the hiring body.
  • Job boards such as RemoteKazi, which list current government and county jobs and link to the official advert.

Always confirm a vacancy on the hiring body's own website before you apply. Scammers regularly post fake government adverts and fake "recruitment lists" on WhatsApp and Facebook.

Step 1: Read the whole advert carefully

Government adverts are detailed, and the panel checks every requirement. Before you apply, note:

  • The job title, reference number and job group or grade. You will need the reference number in your application.
  • Minimum requirements: academic qualifications, professional certificates, years of experience and any registration with a professional body.
  • How to apply: through an online portal, by email, or by post or hand delivery.
  • The deadline, including the time. Late applications are not considered.
  • Any age limits or special rules, for example for internships or security services.

If you do not meet a minimum requirement, such as the years of experience, your application will almost certainly be rejected at the first stage of shortlisting.

Step 2: Prepare your documents

Most government applications need some or all of the following:

  • A tailored CV. See our guide on writing a CV that gets shortlisted.
  • An application letter quoting the job title and reference number. See our cover letter guide.
  • Copies of your national ID, academic and professional certificates, and transcripts
  • Proof of registration with a professional body, where the job requires it
  • Testimonials or letters confirming your previous employment

Scan your documents clearly in colour and save them as PDFs with sensible names before you start, because online portals often have file size limits.

Step 3: Get your Chapter Six clearance documents early

Chapter Six of the Constitution sets standards of leadership and integrity for public officers. For many government jobs, especially senior ones, shortlisted candidates must show clearance from several bodies, including:

  • A certificate of good conduct from the DCI
  • A tax compliance certificate from KRA
  • Clearance from HELB, if you had a student loan
  • Clearance from the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC)
  • A report from a credit reference bureau

These can take days or weeks to process, so start before you are shortlisted. Our guide to clearance documents for government jobs explains how to get each one.

Step 4: Apply through the right channel

Use exactly the method the advert asks for:

  • Online portals: the PSC, TSC and many state corporations accept applications only through their own online systems. Create your account early, fill in every section, and upload all the requested documents.
  • Email: use the exact subject line the advert requests, usually the job title and reference number, and attach your documents as PDFs.
  • Post or hand delivery: address the envelope exactly as instructed, write the job title and reference on it, and get proof of delivery or postage.

Apply several days before the deadline. Portals often slow down or crash on the final day.

Step 5: Prepare for shortlisting and the interview

Shortlisted candidates are usually published on the hiring body's website or contacted directly. Check regularly after the deadline. Government interviews are normally panel interviews, scored against the job requirements. Expect questions about:

  • The functions of the ministry, county or organisation
  • The national values and principles of public service in the Constitution
  • Your technical knowledge for the role
  • Examples from your experience, which you can structure with the STAR method in our interview guide

Bring all your original certificates and your ID for verification on the day.

County government jobs

Each county's Public Service Board advertises on the county website and in local and national newspapers. Counties hire across health, agriculture, education (including early childhood teachers), finance, revenue collection and administration. Some positions give preference to residents of the county, so read the advert carefully, but many are open to all Kenyans.

Teaching jobs with the TSC

To be hired as a teacher in a public school, you need to be registered with the TSC and have a TSC number. The TSC advertises recruitment and replacement vacancies, and applications are made online and then processed at county level. Make sure your registration and documents are up to date before vacancies are announced.

Internships in government

The public service runs internship programmes for recent graduates, and many state corporations offer their own internships and attachments. These are a good way into government work. Look out for them on official websites and our internships page.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Missing the deadline or applying through the wrong channel
  • Leaving out the job reference number
  • Uploading unclear or incomplete documents
  • Applying for jobs where you do not meet the minimum requirements
  • Sending a generic CV instead of one that matches the advert

Beware of recruitment scams

Government recruitment is free. Nobody can sell you a "slot", guarantee you a job in the police or the army, or speed up your application for a fee. If someone asks you for money, report them to the police. Read our guide on how to spot a fake job advert.
