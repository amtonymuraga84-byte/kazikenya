Remote work lets you earn from employers anywhere in the world without leaving Kenya. It is also competitive: one remote advert can attract thousands of applicants from many countries. This guide explains which remote jobs suit beginners, what you need to set up, how to find genuine work, and how to get paid and stay on the right side of KRA.

Types of remote work

Remote work comes in three main forms, and it helps to know which one you are applying for:

  • Full-time remote employment: you work for one company on a regular salary, often as a contractor rather than a formal employee.
  • Freelancing: you find clients and complete individual projects, often through platforms such as Upwork or Fiverr.
  • Task-based and contract work: short contracts such as transcription, AI data labelling, online tutoring or survey work, paid per task or per hour.

Remote jobs that suit beginners

  • Customer support: answering emails and chats for international companies. Strong written English matters most.
  • Virtual assistant: managing email, calendars, research and data entry for busy professionals.
  • Content writing and editing: blog posts, product descriptions and social media captions.
  • AI training and data annotation: labelling images, video or text to train AI systems. Many roles need no prior experience, just accuracy.
  • Transcription and translation: especially valuable if you speak Swahili or another local language well.
  • Online tutoring: teaching English, maths or sciences to students abroad.

With more experience, higher-paying remote roles open up in software development, data analysis, design, digital marketing, accounting and project management.

Step 1: Check that you are eligible

Many "remote" jobs are not open to everyone. Read each advert carefully for:

  • Location rules: some roles say "US only" or "EU only" for legal or tax reasons. Apply only where Kenya, Africa or "worldwide" is allowed.
  • Working hours: Kenya is on East Africa Time (UTC+3). That is one to three hours ahead of most of Europe, and seven or eight hours ahead of New York. A US company may need you online in the evening Kenyan time.
  • Language level: many support roles ask for a specific level of English, sometimes written as B2 or C1.

Step 2: Set up your basics

  • A reliable internet connection, ideally fibre at home, with a mobile data bundle as backup.
  • Power backup: a UPS or power bank for your router, because outages happen and missed shifts cost jobs.
  • A working laptop and a headset with a microphone. Most remote jobs cannot be done on a phone.
  • A quiet workspace for calls.
  • A professional email address and an up-to-date LinkedIn profile with a clear photo.

Step 3: Build proof of your skills

A remote employer cannot visit your office or meet you in person, so they rely on evidence. Make it easy for them to trust you:

  • Writers: three to five sample articles on a free blog or Google Drive folder.
  • Developers: a GitHub profile with real projects.
  • Designers: a portfolio on Behance or a simple website.
  • Virtual assistants: a short video introduction and examples of spreadsheets or documents you have organised.
  • Everyone: short online certificates in the tools you use, such as Excel, Google Workspace or Canva.

Start with smaller contracts if you need to. Reviews and references from your first clients make the next job much easier to win.

Step 4: Find and apply for genuine jobs

Look on established remote job boards, company careers pages, LinkedIn and the remote jobs section on RemoteKazi, which lists roles open to people in Kenya. When you apply:

  • Tailor your CV to each role. Our CV guide explains how.
  • Mention that you are in Kenya, your time zone, and the hours you can work.
  • Show that you can work independently, for example by describing a project you managed on your own.
  • Prepare for an online interview. Test your camera, microphone and internet the day before. Our interview guide has more tips.

Step 5: Avoid remote job scams

Remote work attracts more scams than almost any other kind of job. Walk away if:

  • You are asked to pay to start, to "unlock" tasks or to withdraw your earnings.
  • You are "hired" after a text-only chat, with no video call or proper interview.
  • You are sent a payment and asked to send part of it back, or to buy equipment from a supplier they choose.
  • The pay is far higher than similar roles for very simple work.

Step 6: Get paid

Agree how you will be paid before you start work. Common options for people in Kenya include:

  • Direct bank transfer to a Kenyan bank account, in shillings or a dollar account.
  • International payment services such as Payoneer or Wise, which receive payments from abroad and transfer them to your bank or mobile money.
  • PayPal, which can be linked to M-Pesa for withdrawals.
  • Payment through the freelancing platform itself, if you found the work there.

Compare each option's transfer fees and exchange rates, because the difference can be several percent of your income. Agree the currency, the payment dates and what happens if a payment is late, and keep the agreement in writing.

Step 7: Handle your taxes

If you live in Kenya, income you earn from remote work is generally taxable in Kenya, even when the employer is abroad. Foreign employers usually do not deduct Kenyan tax for you, so you are responsible for it:

  • Make sure you have a KRA PIN. Our post on getting a KRA PIN online explains how.
  • Keep records of every payment you receive and any work-related costs, such as internet and equipment.
  • Declare your income when you file your annual return on iTax, by 30 June each year.

Tax rules can be complex, so speak to a tax adviser or KRA if you are unsure how your income should be declared.

Keep your remote job

  • Be reliable: be online at the agreed hours, and tell your manager early if power or internet fails.
  • Over-communicate: give regular short updates on what you have done and what you are working on.
  • Meet deadlines, and ask questions early rather than guessing.
  • Protect your health: keep regular hours, take breaks, and avoid working late into the night every day.
