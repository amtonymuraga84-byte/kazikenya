Nairobi is one of the world's major hubs for international organisations. It hosts the United Nations Office at Nairobi, the headquarters of UNEP and UN-Habitat, and regional offices for hundreds of NGOs, research institutes and donor agencies. These organisations offer meaningful work, competitive pay and good benefits, but their recruitment works quite differently from local companies. This guide explains how to find and apply for NGO and UN jobs from Kenya.

Types of organisations that hire

  • United Nations agencies such as UNEP, UN-Habitat, UNICEF, UNHCR, WFP, WHO and UNDP
  • International NGOs working in humanitarian aid, health, education, livelihoods and the environment
  • Local NGOs and community-based organisations, often working with international partners
  • Research institutes, for example in agriculture, health and the environment
  • Foundations and donor agencies that fund development programmes

Understand UN job categories

UN job adverts use categories that tell you who can apply and how the job is paid:

  • General Service (G) positions are recruited locally. They are usually open to Kenyan nationals and residents, and include administrative, finance, IT, driver and support roles. Grades run from G-1 to G-7.
  • National Professional Officer (NO) positions are professional roles reserved for nationals of the country. In Kenya, these are open only to Kenyan citizens. Grades run from NO-A to NO-D.
  • International Professional (P) positions are open to candidates of all nationalities and can involve working in any duty station.
  • Consultancies and individual contracts are short-term assignments for specific projects.
  • UN Volunteers and internships are good entry routes for younger professionals.

As a Kenyan, General Service and National Officer roles in Nairobi or other Kenyan duty stations are usually your strongest opportunities.

Where to find NGO and UN jobs

  • The UN Careers portal for UN Secretariat jobs, including UNEP and UN-Habitat, and each agency's own careers website for UNICEF, WFP, UNHCR and others
  • ReliefWeb, which lists humanitarian and development jobs from many organisations
  • The careers pages of NGOs you would like to work for. Check them regularly.
  • LinkedIn, where many NGOs advertise and recruiters search for candidates

What NGOs and the UN look for

International organisations score candidates against the exact requirements in the advert. Typical requirements include:

  • Education: a relevant degree. Professional roles often require a master's, although a bachelor's degree with extra years of experience may be accepted.
  • Relevant experience: often counted in years, and sometimes specifically at the national or international level.
  • Technical skills in the field, such as monitoring and evaluation, grants management, public health or finance.
  • Languages: fluent English is essential. Swahili and other local languages are a strong advantage for community-facing roles, and French or Arabic can help for regional roles.
  • Competencies and values, such as integrity, teamwork, respect for diversity and accountability.

How to apply successfully

Match the advert precisely

Many organisations screen applications against the advert's requirements, sometimes with software. Use the same key terms as the advert, and show clearly how you meet each requirement.

Complete the application form fully

The UN and many NGOs use their own online profile or form instead of, or as well as, a CV. Fill in every section in detail, including specific dates and responsibilities for each job. An incomplete profile is often screened out automatically.

Show results

Describe what changed because of your work: "managed a programme reaching 4,000 households" or "reduced reporting delays from four weeks to one". Our CV guide shows how.

Write a specific cover letter

Explain why you want to work for this organisation in particular, and how your experience fits the role. See our cover letter guide.

The recruitment process

NGO and UN recruitment can take weeks or even months. After shortlisting, expect one or more of these:

  • A written test or technical assessment, often timed and online
  • A competency-based interview, where every question asks for a real example from your experience. Prepare several stories using the STAR method in our interview guide.
  • Reference checks, so ask your referees in advance

Do not stop applying elsewhere while you wait for a result.

How to get a foot in the door

  • Internships: the UN and many NGOs run internship programmes, some unpaid, so check the conditions before applying.
  • UN Volunteers: national UN Volunteer assignments are open to Kenyans and give valuable UN experience.
  • Local NGOs: experience at a smaller organisation is often the stepping stone to a larger one.
  • Project and field roles: data collection, enumeration and field officer jobs build experience in programme work.
  • Skills in demand: monitoring and evaluation, data analysis, grant writing, finance and procurement are needed across the sector.

Beware of fake NGO and UN job offers

Scammers often use the names of the UN and well-known NGOs. The UN and genuine NGOs never charge any fee at any stage of recruitment, and offers do not arrive from free email addresses or WhatsApp out of the blue. Read our guide on how to spot a fake job advert, and always apply through the organisation's official careers website.
