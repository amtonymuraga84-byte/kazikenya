A partially built 16-storey building collapsed in Eastleigh, Nairobi, on October 20, 2026. Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen has announced a multi-agency investigation, and police have warned that anyone found to have approved or supervised the project negligently could face criminal charges.

## The NCA under scrutiny

The collapse came just seven weeks after a new leadership team took over at the National Construction Authority (NCA), following the departure of former head Maurice Akech. The authority's Acting Executive Director, Susan C. Deritu, took office on September 2, 2026. The investigation is expected to look at how the project was registered, how it was monitored on site, and how quality checks were carried out. No findings have been announced yet.

## Who approves a building in Kenya?

Building approval involves several bodies, and the investigation will look at how each did its part:

- **County governments** approve building plans and issue construction permits. In Nairobi, this is done by the county's planning and building control teams.
- **The National Construction Authority (NCA)** registers contractors, registers construction projects, and monitors sites for compliance with building standards. It also accredits and certifies skilled construction workers and site supervisors.
- **The National Environment Management Authority (NEMA)** handles environmental approvals for larger projects.
- **Professionals on the project**, including architects, structural engineers and quantity surveyors, are responsible for design and supervision and are registered with their own professional boards.

Collapses usually happen when one or more of these checks fails: plans are changed without approval, extra floors are added, poor-quality materials are used, or work goes ahead without qualified supervision.

## Careers that keep buildings safe

Every building collapse is a reminder that construction safety depends on trained people. These are some of the careers involved, and they are in steady demand as Kenya's cities grow:

| Role | What they do | Typical route in |
|---|---|---|
| Structural engineer | Designs the frame and checks that a building can carry its loads | Civil/structural engineering degree, registration with the Engineers Board of Kenya |
| Building inspector | Checks that work on site matches approved plans and standards | Diploma or degree in building or civil engineering, county or NCA roles |
| Quantity surveyor | Plans costs and materials, and checks quantities and quality | Degree in quantity surveying, registration with BORAQS |
| Site supervisor / clerk of works | Supervises daily work and quality on site | Diploma plus site experience, NCA accreditation |
| Health and safety officer | Manages safety on construction sites | Diploma in occupational safety and health, DOSHS registration |
| Skilled tradesperson | Masonry, steel fixing, concrete work and more | TVET training plus NCA accreditation |

TVET colleges across Kenya offer certificates and diplomas in building technology, civil engineering and plumbing, and NCA accreditation can help skilled workers earn more and qualify for bigger projects.

## Stay safe as a buyer or tenant

If you are renting or buying in a new building, ask whether the developer and contractor are registered with the NCA and whether the plans were approved by the county. Be cautious about buildings that appear to have more floors than neighbouring approved developments.
