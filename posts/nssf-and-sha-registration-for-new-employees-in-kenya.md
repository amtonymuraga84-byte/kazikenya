Starting your first job is exciting until the payslip arrives and the take-home pay is lower than the offer letter. Part of the gap is tax, but a good part is statutory deductions: **NSSF** (pension), **SHIF** (health cover through the Social Health Authority) and the **Affordable Housing Levy**. Registering properly in your first month means these deductions work for you instead of disappearing into an account you cannot find.

## What each deduction is

**NSSF (National Social Security Fund)** is a compulsory retirement savings scheme. Both you and your employer pay in, and the money builds up in your personal account for retirement.

**SHIF (Social Health Insurance Fund)** is the health cover run by the **Social Health Authority (SHA)**. According to SHA, contributions are 2.75% of gross monthly income with a minimum of Ksh 300 a month, and for salaried people the employer deducts it from your gross salary and remits it to SHA.

**Affordable Housing Levy** is a payroll levy. KRA says each employee pays 1.5% of gross monthly pay and the employer matches it.

## NSSF: how much is deducted in 2026

NSSF contributions are 6% from the employee and 6% from the employer, charged on pay between a lower and an upper earnings limit. A notice to employers on the "Year 4" rates, effective February 2026, gives a lower earnings limit of **Ksh 9,000** and an upper earnings limit of **Ksh 108,000**. The most an employee can pay in a month is Ksh 6,480, matched by the employer, so Ksh 12,960 in total.

Rates change in phases, so look at the latest NSSF notice before you calculate your own deduction.

## How to register for NSSF as a new employee

Check first whether you are already a member. If you have worked, even briefly, you may have an NSSF number and should reuse it. If not, NSSF's page for employed members says you register at an NSSF office near you. Take:

- Your **original national ID** (or Alien card or passport) and a copy
- An **introduction letter from your employer**

NSSF records your details, registers you and issues a membership card. Your employer needs the NSSF number to pay contributions into your account.

## How to register for SHA

SHA says you can register by dialling ***147#**, or through the SHA or Afyayangu portal, using your national ID number and first name for verification. You can also visit a Huduma Centre or an authorised SHA agent with your ID. You can list your spouse and children as dependants when you register.

Many Kenyans registered during the earlier rollout, so try the USSD code or the member portal first. If you are already registered, you do not need to start again. Give your employer your SHA details so the deductions go to your account.

## Step-by-step checklist for your first month

1. **Get your KRA PIN** if you do not have one. See our guide to [getting a KRA PIN online](/blog/how-to-get-a-kra-pin-online-in-kenya/).
2. **Ask HR what they need.** Most employers ask for your ID, KRA PIN, NSSF number and SHA details.
3. **Ask your employer for an introduction letter** and visit an NSSF office to register if you have no number.
4. **Confirm or create your SHA registration** via *147# or the member portal.
5. **Give your numbers to HR** in writing and keep a copy.
6. **Check your first payslip.** Look for NSSF, SHIF and housing levy lines and see that they match the rules above.
7. **Check your accounts after two months** to confirm contributions appear.

## Why employer remittance matters

Employers must pay NSSF contributions by the 9th day of the following month, and late payments attract penalties. KRA also says the housing levy is due by the 9th. If a deduction appears on your payslip but never shows in your NSSF or SHA account, ask HR first, then contact the institution. It is your money.

## Tax and your payslip

KRA lists contributions to the Social Health Insurance Fund among amounts deductible when working out taxable employment income. Your PAYE therefore takes SHIF into account. Ask HR to explain any line on your payslip you do not understand.

## FAQ

**Do I register myself or does my employer do it?** For NSSF, the official employed-member page describes the employee registering at an NSSF office with an employer letter. For SHA, individuals can register themselves; employers also have their own registration process for deductions.

**Can I opt out?** NSSF and the other deductions are set by law for employees. Check the official pages or ask the institution if you believe you are exempt.

**What if I change jobs?** Keep the same NSSF and SHA numbers. Give them to your new employer.

**Is NHIF still used?** SHA now runs social health insurance. If you hold an old NHIF number, ask SHA whether your records were migrated.

**Where do I get help?** SHA lists a toll-free number, 0800 720 601, and NSSF offices serve members in person.
