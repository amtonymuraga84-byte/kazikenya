Many Kenyan graduates hear the same thing at interviews: "Do you have experience?" An internship is the most common answer. It gives you work history, a referee, and sometimes a professional registration requirement ticked off. This guide explains how internships are advertised in Kenya, how to apply for the government programme, and how to avoid wasting time or money.

## What the Public Service Internship Programme is

The Public Service Internship Programme (PSIP) is run by the Public Service Commission (PSC). According to the Commission, it was first rolled out in the 2019/2020 financial year to give graduates the chance to build technical and professional skills, improve their employability and receive a stipend while they learn.

Interns are placed in ministries, state departments and other public bodies. In June 2026 the PSC inducted 8,286 interns under Cohort VIII, and said the programme has placed 37,504 interns since 2019.

One point the Commission stresses: the programme **was never intended to be a mechanism for automatic employment** in government. Public jobs are filled through competitive, merit-based recruitment. Former interns are encouraged to apply when vacancies open, but an internship does not guarantee a job.

## Who can apply

The PSC's internship guidelines (October 2019) describe the main requirements. Each advert can add its own conditions, so treat the advert as the final word. In general you should be:

- A **Kenyan graduate** from a recognised institution, with a degree or diploma
- **Unemployed**, and not someone who has retired or left formal employment
- A recent graduate: the guidelines say within the last three years, and past adverts have instead named a minimum graduation year
- Someone who has **not already benefited** from a similar internship programme

Adverts also tell you which disciplines are being recruited and sometimes ask for computer skills. Read them line by line before you apply.

## How long it lasts and what you earn

The guidelines set the internship at **12 months**, or the period set by a professional body where one applies. Interns receive a **monthly stipend**, and a subsistence allowance when working out of station. The PSC web pages do not publish the amount, so check the current advert or the PSC for the rate rather than trusting figures on social media.

## Documents to prepare

The guidelines list a valid certificate of good conduct, bio-data, copies of your national ID or passport, and your KRA PIN, plus passport photos. Medical cover is also expected. Start early on the slower items:

- Your [KRA PIN](/blog/how-to-get-a-kra-pin-online-in-kenya/)
- A [certificate of good conduct](/blog/how-to-get-a-certificate-of-good-conduct-in-kenya/), which can take weeks
- Scanned copies of your degree or diploma certificate, transcript and ID

## Step-by-step: applying for PSIP

1. **Go to the PSC website** at publicservice.go.ke and open the Internship page.
2. **Open the active adverts** through the "Apply online" button, which takes you to the PSC recruitment portal.
3. **Create a profile** on the portal using your own email and phone number, then complete your education and personal details.
4. **Read the current advert**: positions, disciplines, graduation year and the closing date.
5. **Upload your documents** in the format asked and submit before the deadline.
6. **Watch for the shortlist and appointment lists.** The PSC publishes shortlisted applicants and lists of appointed interns on its website.

Adverts are not open all year, so check the PSC page regularly and follow the Commission's official channels.

## Other places to find internships

Government is not the only route. Many banks, telecoms, NGOs, county governments, hospitals, law firms and engineering firms take interns, often through their own careers pages. Some professional bodies also require supervised internship or pupillage before you can register. Our [internships page](/internships-kenya/) lists openings we find, but always apply through the employer's own page.

## Protect yourself from scams

Genuine public service internship applications go through official portals. Be careful if someone asks you to pay for a slot, sends you a link on WhatsApp that is not on the official domain, or promises a placement through a "contact" inside an office. Check any advert against the PSC website before sharing your ID or paying anyone.

## Make your application stronger

Use a clean one-page CV and a short cover letter, make sure your names match your ID on every document, and use an email address you check daily. If you are shortlisted, respond quickly. Missing a deadline for documents is a common reason good applicants drop out.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the internship paid?**
Yes, the PSC says the programme pays a stipend. The amount is not published on its web pages, so check the current advert.

**Will I get a job after the internship?**
Not automatically. The PSC says the programme is not a route to automatic employment, though the experience helps your CV.

**Can I apply if I graduated long ago?**
Probably not through PSIP, because the guidelines require a recent graduate. Look at private-sector and NGO internships or volunteering instead.

**Can I do PSIP twice?**
The guidelines exclude people who have benefited from a similar programme.

**Do I pay anything to apply?**
The official application is made online through the PSC portal. Be suspicious of anyone asking for money.
