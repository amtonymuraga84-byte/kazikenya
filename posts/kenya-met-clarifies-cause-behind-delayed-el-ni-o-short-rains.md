Addressing concerns regarding prolonged dry spells across several regions, Hannah Kimani, Director of Public Weather Services at the Kenya Meteorological Department (KMS), explained that low-pressure weather systems forming in the northern Indian Ocean temporarily stalled the southward migration of the Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ).
The ITCZ is the primary weather belt responsible for driving Kenya's October–December short rains as it migrates from the northern hemisphere toward the south.

Under standard conditions, the ITCZ brings early rainfall to western Kenya by the first week of October, while coastal areas often experience rain onset as early as September.   However, the development of northern ocean depressions interrupted this transition:

"We started having low-pressure systems developing over the northern Indian Ocean, and this temporarily stopped the southward progression of the Intertropical Convergence Zone," 
She added that this atmospheric shift diverted moisture away, resulting in persistent dry conditions during a timeframe when parts of the country usually receive significant rainfall. As weather patterns adjust, meteorologists continue to monitor atmospheric conditions to advise the public on upcoming rainfall developments.
