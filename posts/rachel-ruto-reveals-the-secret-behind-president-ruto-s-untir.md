At a national prayer and thanksgiving service on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, First Lady Rachel Ruto answered a question many Kenyans have asked: how does President William Ruto keep up such a punishing schedule?

## What she said

The First Lady said that while some people wonder why the President never seems tired, she believes his energy comes from God and from his commitment to serving Kenyans. She stressed the place of prayer in their family and in government, and asked citizens to keep praying for the country and its leaders.

## Long hours are normal for many Kenyans

The President is not the only one with a demanding schedule. Many Kenyan workers start early, face long commutes in Nairobi traffic, and add an evening side hustle to make ends meet. Purpose and faith help many people through demanding seasons. But long hours over months or years also carry real risks, from poor sleep to burnout, and they can quietly hurt your health, relationships and work performance.

## Signs you may be heading for burnout

- You feel exhausted even after a weekend off
- You dread starting work and feel cynical about your job
- Your concentration and the quality of your work are slipping
- You are getting sick more often, or sleeping badly
- You are relying more on caffeine, alcohol or late-night scrolling to cope

## Practical ways to protect your energy

1. **Guard your sleep.** Most adults need seven to nine hours. A regular bedtime does more for energy than any energy drink.
2. **Use your commute well.** Rest, listen to something you enjoy, or learn a skill through podcasts, rather than starting work on your phone.
3. **Take real breaks.** Short breaks away from your desk, and lunch away from your screen, help you focus for longer.
4. **Move every day.** A 20–30 minute walk improves mood and stamina.
5. **Plan your hardest work for your best hours.** Most people think most clearly in the morning.
6. **Set limits on side hustles.** Extra income helps, but not if it leaves you too tired to do well in your main job.
7. **Use your leave.** Kenyan employees are entitled to annual leave under the Employment Act. Taking it is not a weakness.
8. **Lean on your support system.** Faith, family and friends help many people get through hard seasons. If stress becomes overwhelming, talk to a doctor or counsellor; many employers offer free counselling through medical cover.

## When it's time for a change

Sometimes the problem is the job itself: unrealistic targets, unpaid overtime or a toxic workplace. If that is your situation, it may be time to look elsewhere.
