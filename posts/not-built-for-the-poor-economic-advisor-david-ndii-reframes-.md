In a series of statements published on Saturday, October 24, 2026, David Ndii addressed public misconceptions surrounding the national housing drive. He clarified that the program's primary ambition is to foster an equitable, middle-class society where quality living amenities are accessible across economic tiers.

"Affordable housing is not for the poor. It's inclusive middle-class housing that enables low-income people to enjoy same standard of living with the rest of us, including enabling their children to swim."

He emphasized the concept of social equality ("Usawa ni sawa"), arguing that state interventions should focus on elevating living standards overall rather than isolating low-income earners into distinct economic brackets.

Ndii’s remarks come on the heels of price increases enacted on October 1, 2026, which saw Boma Yangu housing rates rise by up to 20%:   

Studio Units: Adjusted upward from KSh 1.5M to KSh 1.8M.   

One-Bedroom Units: Increased from KSh 2.0M to KSh 2.4M.   

Two-Bedroom Units: Increased from KSh 3.0M to KSh 3.6M.   

Market-driven housing under the state initiative also recorded a 10% hike, pushing two-bedroom homes to KSh 3.96M and three-bedroom units to KSh 5.28M.

The debate over housing costs coincides with recent expenditure surveys across 3,435 households, illustrating the tight budgets Kenyan families navigate:   

Food & Groceries: 52% of income   

Rent Payments: 9% of income   

School Fees & Transport: 6% each   

Utilities & Energy: 5%   

Other Miscellaneous Needs: 22%   

These figures highlight the financial juggling required by households as the government encourages citizens to transition into property ownership.
