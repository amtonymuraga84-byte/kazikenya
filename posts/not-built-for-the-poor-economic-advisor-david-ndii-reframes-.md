David Ndii, President William Ruto's chief economic adviser, has sparked debate with a blunt description of the government's flagship housing project. "Affordable housing is not for the poor," he wrote on October 24, 2026, describing it instead as middle-class housing that lets lower-income families live to the same standard as everyone else. He summed up the idea of equality behind it with the phrase "Usawa ni sawa".

His remarks come at a sensitive time. On October 1, 2026, prices for units sold through the Boma Yangu platform went up by as much as 20%.

## The new prices

| Unit type | Old price | New price |
|---|---|---|
| Studio | KSh 1.5 million | KSh 1.8 million |
| One bedroom | KSh 2.0 million | KSh 2.4 million |
| Two bedroom | KSh 3.0 million | KSh 3.6 million |

Market-rate units under the same programme rose by about 10%, taking a two-bedroom home to around KSh 3.96 million and a three-bedroom home to about KSh 5.28 million.

## Why people are reacting

Many Kenyans pay the Affordable Housing Levy, which is deducted at 1.5% of gross salary, with employers adding another 1.5%. Some workers feel that if they are funding the programme, the homes should be within reach of low earners. Ndii's argument is different: the programme aims to build good-quality, mixed-income neighbourhoods rather than separate housing for the poor, and the price reflects that standard.

Both sides have a point worth weighing. Higher standards mean better homes and services; higher prices mean fewer workers can qualify.

## How the prices compare with a typical budget

A household spending survey of 3,435 families gives a picture of where Kenyan incomes go:

- Food and groceries: 52%
- Rent: 9%
- School fees: 6%
- Transport: 6%
- Utilities and energy: 5%
- Everything else: 22%

With more than half of income going to food, saving a deposit is the hardest step for many families. As a rough guide, many lenders and housing schemes expect monthly payments to stay below about a third of household income. On a tenant-purchase or mortgage plan, a KSh 1.8 million studio can cost far more in total than its price once interest and fees are added, so it is important to ask for the full repayment schedule before you commit.

## If you are thinking of buying through Boma Yangu

- **Register on the official Boma Yangu platform** only. Do not pay agents who claim they can "secure" a unit for you.
- **Read the payment terms** for each option, such as cash purchase, tenant purchase or mortgage, and ask how long it takes to own the unit.
- **Check your levy deductions** on your payslip; your contributions are recorded against your ID.
- **Plan your deposit** alongside other goals like an emergency fund.
