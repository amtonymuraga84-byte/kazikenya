Before you send a job application, check your own papers. Many employers, universities and government bodies now confirm certificates with the body that issued them, and a lost, damaged or unrecognised certificate can hold up an offer for weeks. This guide explains how KCSE certificates can be accessed and verified, and when a degree needs recognition from a Kenyan authority.

## Why verification matters

Forged certificates are a known problem, so recruiters are careful. Verification protects honest applicants too: if your records are in order and easy to check, you move faster than someone who must "go and look for the certificate" after an interview.

## KCSE and KCPE certificates: the KNEC e-certificate

The Kenya National Examinations Council (KNEC) runs an **E-Certificate Platform** at ecertificate.knec.ac.ke. According to KNEC's user guide (version 2.0, June 2026), it covers **KCSE and KCPE certificates from 1989 to the present**, provided a record exists in KNEC's database.

An e-certificate is a PDF that carries a digital verification hash, so a third party can check that it has not been altered. It is useful if you have lost your paper certificate, if your school is withholding it, or if an employer asks for a quick digital copy.

### Step by step: get your e-certificate

1. Open the E-Certificate Platform and choose **Sign Up**, then select **Applicant**.
2. Enter your name and email. A one-time password (OTP) is sent to your email; it expires after 10 minutes.
3. Complete your profile: citizenship, name as on your national ID, date of birth, phone number and ID number. Your details are checked against the national population register.
4. Click **Generate E-Certificate**. Enter your **examination index number** (not your ID number), choose KCSE or KCPE, and pick the exam year.
5. If the identity check and the record lookup both pass, pay the fee by M-Pesa or another eCitizen method.
6. Download the PDF from **My Certificates**.

KNEC's guide states the generation fee as **KES 1,200 plus VAT and eCitizen charges**. News reports have quoted different amounts, so confirm what the portal shows before you pay. The guide also says the PDF stays downloadable for **six months** after issue; save a copy safely and do not rely on re-downloading later.

## How an employer or school verifies your certificate

KNEC describes two routes:

- **You send a verification report.** From your account you can send a verification report to a named recipient, such as an employer's HR officer. The recipient's details are entered and a payment is made through eCitizen.
- **The organisation verifies directly.** Employers, universities and professional bodies can register a verifier account (with their registration certificate, approved by KNEC) and submit candidates' details and certificate PDFs for checking. Results come back as "Genuine" or "Referred to KNEC".

KNEC's guide does not list the verification fees, so check the portal for current charges.

If you are asked for a "confirmation of results" letter or your certificate is not on the system, contact KNEC directly through the official website, knec.ac.ke.

## Degrees and diplomas from Kenyan institutions

For a qualification from a Kenyan university or college, the usual route is simple: employers ask for your certificate and transcript, and may confirm them with the awarding institution. Keep your original certificate, transcript and graduation documents safe, and ask your institution's registrar or academic office how it handles verification requests. Procedures and charges differ by institution.

It is also wise to confirm that your institution is authorised. The Commission for University Education (CUE) publishes the list of universities authorised to operate in Kenya at cue.or.ke.

## Qualifications from outside Kenya

If you studied abroad, an employer or university in Kenya may ask for formal recognition.

- **CUE recognition and equation.** CUE recognises and equates certificates, diplomas, degrees and postgraduate awards from foreign universities through its online portal. Its guidelines ask for certified copies of certificates and transcripts and a copy of your national ID or passport, and documents in other languages need an English translation. The guidelines list fees that depend on who applies and whether you want express service, so read the current schedule on the portal before paying.
- **KNQA alignment and validation.** The Kenya National Qualifications Authority (KNQA) aligns and validates foreign qualifications into the Kenya National Qualifications Framework through its online portal. It lists separate fees for alignment and validation, and no original documents are submitted for evaluation.

Which body applies to you can depend on your qualification and your employer, so ask the employer or institution which one they want.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Waiting until you have a job offer to look for a lost certificate.
- Using a different spelling of your name than the one on your ID.
- Paying "agents" who promise to get certificates quickly. Use only official portals.
- Using a fake or altered certificate. It can end an application and lead to criminal charges.

## FAQ

**Do I need an e-certificate if I still have my paper certificate?** Not always, but a digital copy is convenient for online applications and quick checks.

**Can I get a KCSE certificate from before 1989?** KNEC's guide says the system covers 1989 onwards. For earlier years, contact KNEC directly.

**Does my degree need CUE recognition?** CUE's portal is for foreign-awarded qualifications. Kenyan graduates usually only need their institution's documents, unless an employer says otherwise.

**Who pays for verification?** It depends on the route: you pay when you send a verification report, and an organisation pays when it verifies on its own account.
