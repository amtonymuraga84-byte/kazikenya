A TikTok livestream has started a national conversation. Tallie, the mother of one of media personality Oga Obinna's children, broke down in tears while asking followers to help her raise money to start a business she could run from home.

## What happened

Tallie told viewers that raising a child alone had been financially hard. She said that even with a degree in journalism she had not been able to find steady work, so she wanted to start a small business that would let her earn while caring for her daughter. Without starting capital, she turned to the public for help.

Clips spread quickly, and many people asked why Obinna had not helped. He responded that he fully provides for his daughter, including her upkeep and schooling, but that funding her mother's business is not his responsibility. He said child support is for the child, and wished Tallie success.

Online, opinion split. Some argued a father who fully provides for his child has done his part. Others said helping the mother become financially stable would ultimately benefit the child too.

## The bigger issue: graduates without jobs

Whatever side you take, one part of the story is familiar to many families: a graduate with a good qualification who cannot find a job. Kenyan universities and colleges produce far more graduates each year than there are formal jobs. Many people end up combining part-time work, freelancing and small businesses to make a living.

If you are in that position, a home business can be a practical step, especially if you also care for children. The key is to start small, use skills you already have, and keep costs low.

## 8 home businesses you can start with little capital

1. **Freelance writing and editing.** Ideal for journalism and communication graduates. Start by writing for local businesses, blogs and NGOs, then build a portfolio for international clients.
2. **Social media management.** Many small businesses need someone to post, reply to messages and run simple ads. A phone, data and a few sample accounts are enough to start.
3. **Online tutoring.** Teach CBC subjects, KCSE revision or languages over video calls or in your neighbourhood.
4. **Transcription and virtual assistance.** Remote clients pay for typing up recordings, managing email and scheduling. Accuracy and reliability matter more than equipment.
5. **Home baking or packed meals.** Sell to neighbours, offices and schools. Check county food-handling rules and get a medical certificate if required.
6. **Thrift (mitumba) reselling online.** Buy selected clothes in bulk and sell through WhatsApp status, TikTok and Instagram.
7. **Liquid soap, detergents or beauty products.** Low start-up costs and repeat customers, but learn proper safety and labelling first.
8. **Daycare or after-school care.** If you are already at home with a child, caring for one or two others can bring income. Check county licensing rules.

## Before you start

- **Test demand first.** Sell to a few people before spending on stock or equipment.
- **Separate your money.** Keep business takings in a separate M-Pesa or bank account.
- **Look for support.** Government youth and women's funds, county programmes and chamas can provide small loans or grants. Avoid digital lenders with very high interest.
- **Keep applying for jobs.** A business and a job search can run side by side.

## Register your business properly

A registered business is easier to grow and more trusted by customers and suppliers.

1. **Register a business name** through the Business Registration Service on eCitizen.
2. **Get a KRA PIN** if you don't have one, and learn your tax obligations. See our [KRA PIN guide](/blog/how-to-get-a-kra-pin-online-in-kenya/).
3. **Get a single business permit** from your county government if your business needs one.
4. **Check special licences**, for example food handling or health certificates for food businesses.

## How to price what you sell

- **Add up all your costs:** materials, packaging, transport, data and a fair amount for your own time.
- **Check what others charge** for similar products or services in your area or online.
- **Set a price that leaves a profit** after costs. Selling cheaply to attract customers only works if you can still cover your expenses.
- **Track every sale and expense** in a notebook or a free app, so you know whether the business is really making money.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much money do I need to start a home business?**
It depends on the business. Service businesses like writing, tutoring or social media management can start with a phone and data, while baking or reselling needs some stock.

**Do I need to register my small business?**
Registering a business name and getting the right county permit makes it easier to open a business bank account, get contracts and access government funds.

**Where can young people and women get business funding?**
Government funds for youth and women, county programmes, SACCOs and chamas are common sources. Compare interest rates and avoid high-cost digital loans.
