Tallie, the mother of one of Obinna’s children, recently opened up to her followers during an emotional TikTok live session. Speaking through tears, she described the financial strain of raising a child as a single mother.

She explained that despite holding a degree in journalism, she has struggled to secure steady employment. Her plan is to start a small business she can run from home, allowing her to earn an income while caring for her daughter. Without the capital to get started, she turned to the public for support.

Clips of the livestream spread quickly across social media, and many viewers directed their questions at Obinna, asking why the father of her child had not stepped in to help.

Responding to the backlash, Obinna made it clear that he takes his role as a father seriously. He said he consistently caters to his daughter’s needs, including her upkeep and schooling, and that she lacks nothing on his end.

However, he distinguished between supporting his child and financing her mother. In his view, child support exists for the benefit of the child, while the mother’s personal bills and business ambitions remain her own responsibility as an adult. He added that he wishes Tallie success and hopes she finds a way to achieve her goals.

The exchange has triggered a heated debate online, with Kenyans split into two camps:

Team Obinna: Many users argued that a father who fully provides for his child has fulfilled his duty and should not be pressured into funding an ex-partner’s plans.

Team Tallie: Others felt that helping the mother become financially stable would ultimately improve the child’s quality of life, making it a shared interest.

The situation has reignited wider conversations about co-parenting in Kenya, particularly where the boundaries lie between supporting a child and supporting a former partner. As public figures increasingly share personal struggles online, such disputes are playing out in full view of millions.

For now, both parties appear to be moving forward on their own terms, with Tallie still seeking support for her business idea.
