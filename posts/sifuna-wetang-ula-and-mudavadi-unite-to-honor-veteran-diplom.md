In a rare show of unity, Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna, National Assembly Speaker Moses Wetang'ula and Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi attended the requiem mass of veteran diplomat and former Cabinet Minister Mzee Burudi Nabwera on Wednesday. The service was held at Friends Church on Ngong Road, Nairobi, and Senator Sifuna later shared photos from the gathering on social media.

## A career of firsts

Nabwera, who died on October 8, 2026, had a long career in public service:

- **Kenya's first ambassador to the United States**
- **Kenya's first permanent representative to the United Nations**
- **Minister of State in the Office of the President**
- **The first MP for Lugari Constituency** when it was created in 1988

Tributes have come from leaders across the political divide, as well as members of the public who remembered his role in representing a newly independent Kenya on the world stage.

## Farewell arrangements

The Nairobi service was the first of several ceremonies planned by the family. A second requiem service was set for Friday, October 23, at Friends Church in Kiminini, ahead of the burial on Saturday, October 24, at his home in Mlimani, Lugari, Kakamega County.

## Politics set aside

The joint appearance stood out because the three leaders have recently taken different positions on political alliances and plans for the 2027 General Election. At the church, however, the focus stayed on Nabwera's life and his contribution to the country.

## Want a career in diplomacy? Here is how it works today

Nabwera's generation built Kenya's foreign service from scratch. Today it is a structured career path, and there are several ways in.

**1. The Foreign Service (Ministry of Foreign and Diaspora Affairs).** Foreign service officers are recruited through public adverts, usually published by the Public Service Commission and on the ministry's website. Officers start at headquarters in Nairobi and can later be posted to embassies and high commissions abroad.

**2. International organisations based in Kenya.** Nairobi hosts the UN Office at Nairobi, UNEP and UN-Habitat, along with many international NGOs. These offer internships, national officer posts and international roles.

**3. Regional bodies.** The East African Community, African Union and other regional organisations recruit professionals from member states.

**Useful qualifications and skills:**

- A degree in international relations, law, economics, political science or a related field
- Strong writing, negotiation and public speaking skills
- A second foreign language such as French, Arabic, Portuguese or Chinese
- Internship or volunteer experience with government, the UN or an NGO

Start by watching the [UN Careers](https://careers.un.org/) site and the [Public Service Commission](https://www.publicservice.go.ke/) for openings, and check the [latest jobs on RemoteKazi](/#jobs), where we list NGO and international roles in Kenya.

## Building a CV for public service and international jobs

Diplomatic and international roles are highly competitive, and the application is often your only chance to stand out.

- **Follow the format exactly.** Many public bodies and UN agencies use their own online forms. Fill in every field fully rather than writing "see CV".
- **Show results, not just duties.** For each role, include one or two concrete achievements, such as a report you wrote, an event you organised or a budget you managed.
- **List your languages honestly,** with your level in each. Interview panels often test them.
- **Prepare for written tests.** Many foreign service and UN recruitments include a written exam or assessment on current affairs, writing and analysis. Reading quality news daily and practising short policy summaries helps.

## Entry routes for graduates

You don't need to start as an ambassador. Common first steps include:

- **Internships** with government ministries, UN agencies in Nairobi and international NGOs. Many UN internships are unpaid or pay only a small stipend, so check the terms before you apply.
- **Graduate and public service internship programmes**, advertised by the Public Service Commission on its website.
- **Research and programme assistant roles** at think tanks, embassies' local staff positions and regional organisations.
- **Volunteering** with community and youth organisations, which builds the experience many panels look for.

Keep copies of your [clearance documents](/blog/clearance-documents-for-government-jobs-in-kenya/) up to date; government and many international employers ask for them at short notice.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who was Burudi Nabwera?**
He was a veteran Kenyan diplomat and politician who served as Kenya's first ambassador to the United States, its first permanent representative to the UN, a Minister of State, and the first MP for Lugari.

**What should I study to become a diplomat?**
International relations, law, economics and political science are common choices, but strong writing, analysis and language skills matter as much as the degree.

**Do I need to speak French to work at the UN?**
English is widely used, but a second UN language such as French or Arabic is a strong advantage and is required for some roles.
