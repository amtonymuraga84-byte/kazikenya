Kiharu MP Ndindi Nyoro has stirred fresh debate after telling a rally that the opposition should be open to anyone willing to join its effort to unseat the current government. To make his point, he said that even President William Ruto's own son would deserve a chance if he crossed over.

## What Nyoro said

Speaking at a recent political gathering, Nyoro argued that the opposition cannot afford to turn people away. In his view, the side that brings together the most supporters will be best placed in the next election, so new allies should be welcomed regardless of where they come from. His example of the President's son was meant to show how far that openness should go.

## How people reacted

The comment spread quickly on social media and drew mixed reactions:

- **Supporters** saw it as a practical call for unity, arguing that elections are won with numbers and broad coalitions.
- **Critics** said it showed that political alliances in Kenya are built on convenience rather than shared policies, and questioned whether such a coalition could govern together.
- **Others** treated it as a typical rally line, meant to energise a crowd rather than to describe a real plan.

## Why it matters

Nyoro was once seen as one of President Ruto's closest allies in Parliament. Over recent months he has moved away from the Kenya Kwanza administration and become more openly critical of it, sharing platforms with leaders who oppose the government. His comments reflect a wider trend: with the General Election due in August 2027, politicians on all sides are reshuffling alliances, and loyalties that looked fixed a year ago are now changing.

For ordinary Kenyans, these shifts matter because coalitions decide which policies are pushed in Parliament, how county and national budgets are shared, and which promises reach the ballot. It is worth judging any alliance by what it plans to do on jobs, the cost of living and public services, rather than by who has joined it.

## What to watch next

- Whether more MPs from the ruling coalition publicly cross over before 2027.
- How opposition leaders agree on a single presidential candidate, if they do.
- What concrete plans on youth employment and the economy each side puts forward.

## Election season also brings temporary jobs

The run-up to a general election creates short-term work. The IEBC usually recruits thousands of temporary staff, such as presiding officers, deputy presiding officers and polling clerks, for voter registration and election day. These posts are advertised on the IEBC's official website and in national newspapers. Applications are free; anyone asking you to pay for an election job is running a scam.
